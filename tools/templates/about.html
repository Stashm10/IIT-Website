What this is

The IIT Spectrum Observatory records radio signals from the rooftop of IIT Tower at 10 W. 35th Street, Chicago. This site covers {{num_bands}} frequency bands in {{num_groups}} groups between {{low_mhz}} and {{high_mhz}} MHz for {{year}}.

What one reading is

One reading is the received power, in dBm, at one frequency point during one sweep of the receiver. There are about {{sweeps_per_day}} sweeps per day, roughly one every {{interval_min}} minutes ({{interval_s}} seconds between sweeps).

How the histograms are made

Every reading from every frequency point in a band and every sweep is counted into fixed 0.5 dB bins. A month, quarter or year is the exact sum of its days' histograms; nothing is averaged. Pooled medians and percentiles are read from the summed histogram.

The bands

The {{num_groups}} groups follow Wei & Hood (IEEE RTC 2026). The breakdown inside each group follows the FCC Table of Frequency Allocations and the 700/800 MHz band plans.{{draft_sentence}}

Limitations

  • Power levels are comparable within a band over time, but not always between bands, because different parts of the spectrum were measured with different receiver settings.
  • The frequency step is coarse in some bands: the spacing between frequency points ranges from {{step_min}} kHz to {{step_max}} kHz depending on the part of the spectrum.
  • Some bands contain very few frequency points (as few as {{min_points}}).
  • Some days are missing and some are partial (see Coverage above).
  • The site shows received power. It does not estimate occupancy or identify transmitters.
