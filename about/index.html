IIT

About the data

IIT Spectrum Observatory  ·  Chicago, IL  ·  2018
← Back to Overview

What this is

The IIT Spectrum Observatory records radio signals from the rooftop of IIT Tower at 10 W. 35th Street, Chicago. This site covers 91 frequency bands in 10 groups between 44 and 900 MHz for 2018.

What one reading is

One reading is the received power, in dBm, at one frequency point during one sweep of the receiver. There are about 123 sweeps per day, roughly one every 11.7 minutes (704.6 seconds between sweeps).

How the histograms are made

Every reading from every frequency point in a band and every sweep is counted into fixed 0.5 dB bins. A month, quarter or year is the exact sum of its days' histograms; nothing is averaged. Pooled medians and percentiles are read from the summed histogram.

The bands

The 10 groups follow Wei & Hood (IEEE RTC 2026). The breakdown inside each group follows the FCC Table of Frequency Allocations and the 700/800 MHz band plans. The breakdown inside each group is provisional and awaiting confirmation.

Limitations

  • Power levels are comparable within a band over time, but not always between bands, because different parts of the spectrum were measured with different receiver settings.
  • The frequency step is coarse in some bands: the spacing between frequency points ranges from 2.5 kHz to 36.5 kHz depending on the part of the spectrum.
  • Some bands contain very few frequency points (as few as 6).
  • Some days are missing and some are partial (see Coverage above).
  • The site shows received power. It does not estimate occupancy or identify transmitters.
